Clear Study Notes: Comparative Themes

Use these simplified notes to help you write your Leaving Cert essays. They explain the main themes of the play.

1. Roles of Women & Power

The Rule: Women were treated like property. A daughter belonged to her father until she married, then she belonged to her husband.

The Conflict: Desdemona tries to make her own choices, but society punishes her. Emilia points out that this is unfair and that women have feelings too.

2. Race & Outsiders

The Rule: Venice was a mostly white society. Outsiders were viewed with suspicion.

The Conflict: Othello is a Black man in a white society. He is respected only because he is good at fighting wars. Secretly, characters like Iago use awful racist language against him.

3. Honor & Reputation

The Rule: Your reputation (what people think of you) was the most important thing you owned. Men believed they had to look strong and in control.

The Conflict: Cassio is destroyed when he loses his job and his good name. Othello murders his wife mostly because he is terrified that people will laugh at him for being cheated on.

4. Venice vs. Cyprus (Setting)

The Rule: Venice represents safety, civilization, and strict laws. Problems are solved in court.

The Conflict: Cyprus is an island at war. It is far away from the laws of Venice. When the characters move to Cyprus, the rules break down, and Iago can easily commit his crimes.

5. Rules & Manipulation

The Rule: People are expected to follow the law and be honest.

The Conflict: Iago is a villain who lies and cheats. He knows all the rules of society and uses them against good people. He pretends to be honest to destroy Othello.
